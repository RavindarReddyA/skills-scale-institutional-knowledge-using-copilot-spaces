# OctoAcme Project Management Docs

This directory contains the central, versioned reference for OctoAcme project management practices. The guides describe how teams initiate, plan, execute, communicate, release, and improve cross-functional work.

## Project Management Process Summary

OctoAcme follows a customer-first, iterative delivery lifecycle with clear ownership and data-informed decisions. Projects begin with **initiation**, where the team validates the business need, identifies stakeholders, defines measurable success criteria, establishes an initial timeline, and makes a go/no-go decision for planning. During **planning**, approved work is turned into a prioritized and estimated backlog with acceptance criteria, a Definition of Done, milestones, release plans, risks, and dependencies.

During **execution and tracking**, teams deliver small, testable increments through the project board, regular standups, weekly delivery syncs, pull requests, demos, and progress metrics such as velocity and burndown. Project Managers coordinate schedules, risks, dependencies, and communication; Product Managers define outcomes and priorities; Developers implement and test solutions; QA/Testing validates quality and acceptance criteria; and stakeholders provide input and approvals.

Communication is maintained through regular PM and Product Manager alignment, delivery-team standups, stakeholder updates, and a single source of truth for project status. Risks and dependencies are recorded in a risk register, reviewed regularly, and escalated from the team to the Project Manager, Product Lead, and Sponsor when needed. Quality assurance is built into the workflow through unit tests, integration tests where applicable, end-to-end smoke tests for critical flows, CI checks, security scanning, manual QA when appropriate, and reviewed pull requests that include issue links and acceptance criteria.

Before release, teams confirm that acceptance criteria are met, CI and security checks pass, release notes are prepared, smoke tests are ready, and rollback or mitigation plans are documented. After each sprint, release, milestone, or incident, retrospectives capture what went well, what needs improvement, and actionable follow-ups with owners and due dates. This feedback loop helps OctoAcme reduce risk, improve delivery, and continuously strengthen its processes.
